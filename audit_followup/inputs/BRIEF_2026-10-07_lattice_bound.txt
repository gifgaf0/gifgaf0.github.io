LATTICE-SPACING BOUND BRIEF: how fine must the lattice be for the light window
to stay open?
Base: V4.94. This computes a bound. It is not a verdict on anything already
decided.

WHY
Phase C left the polycrystal floor open.
- The light window caps grains at 3.764×10⁻³³ m (W^EM_∪ of record).
- Under the declared chain (ξ = ℓ_P, C = ξ/a ∈ [0.0213, 0.0851]), the lattice
  spacing is 1.9–7.6×10⁻³⁴ m, so a grain holds only 5–20 cells.
- But ξ = ℓ_P is not licensed on the light side. That chain also pins the
  healing length, not the cell, to ℓ_P, which puts the cells at 12–47 Planck
  lengths.
So turn the question around: what is the largest lattice spacing a_max that
all photon data allow, as a function of the minimum cells per grain, N?

HOW TO WORK
- Pre-register the photon arms, the optical-depth threshold and the
  birefringence limit before computing. Do the comparison last.
- One leg is enough. If the window comes out empty for every N ≥ 20 on every
  arm, run a blind second leg on the decisive arm before folding.
- Prior Address first:
  - the homogenization / representative-volume-element literature: how many
    cells a grain needs for effective-medium averaging to hold. Ranganathan &
    Ostoja-Starzewski and Stanke & Kino are already cited;
  - LHAASO's PeV source catalogue;
  - astrophysical bounds on vacuum birefringence (Kostelecký & Mewes and
    successors);
  - LHAASO's quadratic Lorentz-violation bound.
- State the result as a bound ("the data require a ≤ …"), not a prediction.
  Don't re-pin ξ or any other scale after seeing numbers.
- One short fold recording the bound is authorized. Put a plain-language
  summary first. Write to the repo.

   - Compare it with the statistical texture expected from randomly oriented

   This bound does not depend on the lattice spacing.
