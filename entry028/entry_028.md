### Question

Entry 026 found the χ₋₃, χ₋₄ and (·/5) lag-1 correlations at 10¹² above its "full HL" values, by +1.7 to +2.4 s.e. Full HL there was the archived ladder: exact absorption of the primes ≤ 19, and (2.16) at the inflated density λ' = Q'/(φ(Q') log y) for the primes above 19. All 21 decisive tests passed, since the offsets were inside τ.

After the data were seen, three exploratory notes built a fixed-λ control-variate hybrid (see Disclosures). It keeps the primes ≤ 19 exact, and treats the primes above 19 with the k ≤ 6 cumulant expansion at the base density λ₆₀ = 15/(4 log y). At arm E the hybrid lies within 0.16, 0.30 and 0.61 s.e. of the data.

This entry asks, on fresh primes:
1. **T-lad.** Are the correlations above the inflated-λ ladder (Entry 026's through-19 value)?
2. **T-hyb.** Are they consistent with the frozen fixed-λ hybrid?

### Prior address

- Both predictions rest on the Hardy–Littlewood k-tuple conjecture, which is unproved. No theorem decides either outcome. This includes RH and GRH: they place the zeros, but they do not give consecutive-prime correlations at this precision.
- The hybrid's difference from the ladder is a model-side truncation effect, not a new term. Absorbing a prime into Q multiplies λ by p/(p − 1). The ladder treats the primes above its cutoff through (2.16) at that inflated λ, and so carries an O(1/p) drift (Entry 026, ladder steps).
  - At fixed λ the expansion is bounded explicitly: deterministic k ≤ 5, Monte Carlo k = 6, and a k ≥ 7 allowance (claude/q4_cumulant/tail_bound/).
- The hybrid is a model computation fixed before the data. Nothing in it is fitted to the data.

### Predictions (`make_pred_028.py` → `pred_028.json`)

- Each model is evaluated at 4 Gauss–Legendre nodes in log y (dy weight), placed on [1.050215·10¹², 2.049715·10¹²] at y = 1.100126, 1.309538, 1.643818 and 1.956723 ·10¹² (placed in the draft and kept unchanged). ### Statistics and decisive tests (`analyze_028.py`)

- **Estimator.** ĉ_r = Σ χ(p)χ(p′)/n over stratum r's lag-1 pairs; ĉ = mean over r = 0..1999.
- **Standard error.** se_pair = (1/(2P))·√Σ_{j<P}(ĉ_{2j+1} − ĉ_{2j})², P = 1,000. The binomial s.e. √((1 − ĉ²)/N) is reported alongside.
  - A units (ratio) s.e. is also printed. It is report-only and inflated by height drift between strata (dry runs: 1.6–1.9× binomial).
  - **Bias of se_pair** (`sebias_028.py`, n = 1.25·10⁷, 20,000 replicates, 1σ precision 0.016%): with the band's (2.16)-shaped drift, E[se_pair]/se_true − 1 = −0.037%, −0.000%, −0.047%; with the drift ×100, +1.1% to +1.9%, which is the conservative direction. **PASS** (< 0.5%).
- **Tests.** Six decisive tests; α = 0.01, α' = 0.01/6; z₁ = 2.9352 (one-sided), z₂ = 3.1440 (two-sided).
  - **T-lad** (3 cells): z_lad = (ĉ − c_full)/se_pair > 2.9352. The data are above the inflated-λ ladder.
  - **T-hyb** (3 cells): |ĉ − c_hyb| ≤ τ_hyb + 3.1440·se_pair. The data are consistent with the fixed-λ hybrid.

**Planned power.** The planned s.e. is binomial; at Entry 026's arm E the observed s.e. was 0.92–0.98 of binomial.

### Outcomes

- **All six as predicted:** the fresh data sit above the inflated-λ ladder and on the fixed-λ hybrid, for all three characters. Entry 026's arm-E offsets are then attributed to the ladder's λ inflation, now pre-registered.
- **T-lad passes, T-hyb fails:** the data are above the ladder but off the hybrid by more than τ_hyb.
  - If they are beyond the hybrid (less negative), the k ≥ 7 allowance or the HL model itself is suspect.
  - If they are between the two, the hybrid over-corrects.
  - Either way, interpret only in a separate entry.
- **T-lad fails, T-hyb fails:** the data are on the ladder. Entry 026's arm-E offsets were fluctuations or arm-specific.
- **T-lad fails with T-hyb passing:** possible mainly for (·/5), the closest pair. Read as inconclusive for that character.
- The analysis runs once.
