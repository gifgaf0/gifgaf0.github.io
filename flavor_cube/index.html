Flavor Cube

At a vertex v0 of the seven-vertex torus, the six neighbours e1…e6 sit on the faces of a cube, and the 24 symmetries of the up Fano plane that fix v0 move them the way the cube's tetrahedral group moves its faces. Turn the cube by the three mixing angles to see the measured quark and lepton mixing, switch to Vertex for the geometry, or scramble it in Puzzle.

Any sequence of legal turns keeps the total corner twist at 0 mod 3. Twist one corner by hand and the total becomes 1 or 2: then no legal sequence solves the cube. The note compares this rule with triality, where color-neutral states have triality 0. It is an analogy only.

What the cube is

Label the torus's seven vertices by ℤ7 and fix v0 = 0 (Furey's e7 is e0 here). Its 14 triangles are the lines of two Fano planes: the up-triangles {i, i+1, i+3}, which are the octonion lines eiei+1 = ei+3, and the down-triangles {i, i+2, i+3}.

  • The 24 symmetries of the up Fano plane that fix v0 act on its six neighbours exactly as the cube's tetrahedral group Td acts on its faces: 12 turns and 12 mirror moves. No match to the cube's 24 turns alone exists. CHECKED
  • Under the match, each of Furey's three colors zk = er + i e3r is a pair of opposite faces, and QR = {e1, e2, e4} is a corner.
  • The whole Fano plane fits on the cube. The three lines through v0 are the axes. The other four lines are the corners of the tetrahedron inscribed in the cube, whose edges are one diagonal on each face. Turn on “Fano+ lines” in Vertex to see it.
  • The torus itself contributes only the 120° and 240° turns about the QR corner (x ↦ 2x and x ↦ 4x). Its 60°, 180° and 300° turns swap the two Fano planes and are not cube symmetries.
  • Looking down the body diagonal through the QR corner (Corner view), the six faces project onto the weights of the SU(3) triplet and antitriplet. The six silhouette edges join exactly the J5 and J6 partners, alternating.

Mixing on the cube

A real mixing matrix is orthogonal; after flipping the sign of a column if needed (a rephasing), it is a rotation, so it is an orientation of the cube. Its columns say where the cube's three axes point, and |Uij|² is the squared cosine between fixed axis i and the cube's axis j. The standard parametrization is three turns in a row about the fixed rods: θ12 about the third, then θ13 about the second, then θ23 about the first. (About the cube's own axes the order is reversed.)

  • Quark mixing barely turns the cube: θ12 ≈ 13°, θ23 ≈ 2.4°, θ13 ≈ 0.2°. Lepton mixing turns it a lot: θ12 ≈ 34°, θ23 ≈ 49°, θ13 ≈ 8.5°.
  • Tri-bimaximal mixing puts the cube's ν2 axis on a body diagonal of the fixed rods, ν2 = (e + μ − τ)/√3, so its column is (⅓, ⅓, ⅓). Seen down that diagonal, ν1 = (2e − μ + τ)/√6 points along the e rod's shadow. (This is a diagonal of the rods, not the Corner view button, which looks down the cube's own QR corner.) θ13 ≠ 0 is exactly ν1 leaving the e shadow, by about 10° for the measured 8.5°. It need not move ν2: TM2 keeps it on the diagonal, and the measured ν2 is within about 3° of it.
  • E4 is the family-symmetry experiment of the seed, a separate fresh start begun after the program closed (seed/SEED.md). Its F21 and PSL(2,7) act as 3×3 matrices on the three generations, not as the torus's permutations of its seven vertices, and the S4 behind TM1 is not identified with the cube's Td.
  • E4's F21 allows no residual pattern that fits. Its best lepton pattern is no mixing at all (χ² 2459); its best quark pattern has χ² 1.08 × 10⁵. EXCLUDED (E4)
  • In PSL(2,7), 3 of 12 one-column patterns fit, each with two fitted parameters: ℤ7, TM1 and TM2 (the last keeps ν2 on the diagonal). Each needs a ℤ2 of PSL(2,7), which F21 lacks, a vacuum alignment set by hand, and a reason for a family index. Their δ comes out of fits that include NuFIT's δ. FITS (E4)
  • The gauge paper's §6 mismatch is visible on the cube. Each axis (a Fano+ doublet) touches two of the three J5 pairs (the Fano− doublets) and misses the third. Read as |V|² with ½ in each touching cell, the pattern cannot come from any unitary matrix: two rows share only one nonzero column, so they cannot be orthogonal. The mismatch alone gives no mixing matrix. CHECKED

The three axes carry generations here, as in the gauge paper's §4. That is a picture, not a derivation: the note shows Furey's colors use the same three Fano lines.

Three pairings, three hexagons

At v0 the six neighbours can be paired in exactly three ways that the 120° turn x ↦ 2x keeps: J3, J5 and J6. Each gives three fermion modes a(v) = ½(γ(v) + iγ(Jv)). Any two of the pairings join into a hexagon in which the third pairs opposite points:

  • J3 and J5 make the torus link of v0; J6 runs straight through v0 along the three Hamiltonian cycles.
  • J5 and J6 make the cube's corner-view silhouette; J3, Furey's pairing, joins opposite faces.
  • J3 and J6 make a third hexagon, with J5 opposite.

This works because the three pairings together use every QR–QNR pair exactly once; three arbitrary pairings would not do it. Only J3 keeps all of color: in the cyclic labels used here it is Le0, the pairing of Furey's ladder operators. J5 and the cycle pairing J6, which the gauge paper's §5 used, keep the same SU(2), the one fixing r+g+b. Each puts a quarter of its vacuum on Furey's neutrino and three quarters on up-quark states along r+g+b.

What the cube does not show

  • The research program this geometry came from is closed (ledger V4.97). The mathematics carries forward; no physics claim is made here.
  • The cube does not derive any mixing angle. The presets are the measurements, tri-bimaximal mixing, E4's residual-symmetry patterns (three fitted, one not), no mixing, and the gauge paper's two readings of 3/13, all compared with the data E4 used.
  • The gauge paper's 3/13 as sin θC is excluded at 7.6σ (|Vus|) and 8.5σ (global fit) against PDG 2024. WITHDRAWN (v6.4) As tan θC it agrees, but that reading was found after the comparison. POST HOC (Conjecture 3)
  • “Three Fock modes = three Hamiltonian cycles” (§5) does not hold up: the cycle modes keep only an SU(2) of color. FAILS (note)
  • Generations on the three axes, and Fano+/Fano− as left- and right-handed, are the gauge paper's assignments, not results. The same axes carry Furey's colors, and color mixes them.
  • The corner-twist rule and triality share a mod-3 count; nothing more. ANALOGY
