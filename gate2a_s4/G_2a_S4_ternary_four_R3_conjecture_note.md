## The core reading (R2-defensible; pure representation theory)

There are two structurally different 4's in this territory, and the μ_n quartet is **not** the
binary kind:

- **Binary 4's (doubling algebras).** Cayley–Dickson reaches 4 as ℍ = 2² (two doublings). The
  Clifford/Dirac 4 is 2 ⊕ 2 — two Weyl doublets, an *additive* pair. Both are powers/sums of
  two.
- **The quartet is ternary.** Spin-3/2 = **Sym³(ℂ²)**, the symmetric *cube* of the doublet.
  dim Symⁿ(ℂ²) = n+1, so four states ⇒ **exactly three** constituents. The weight ladder
  −3/2, −1/2, +1/2, +3/2 is what three aligned halves span. The "4" is "3+1" in the only sense
  that matters: n=3.

**Schur–Weyl duality makes the three-ness exact on three strands:**

    (ℂ²)^⊗3  =  ( 4 ⊗ triv_{S₃} )  ⊕  ( 2 ⊗ std_{S₃} )

The quartet is precisely the sector on which the permutation group S₃ of the *three* tensor
factors acts **trivially** (totally symmetric); the two doublets carry the standard 2-dim rep of
S₃ (mixed symmetry). So the quartet is the Schur–Weyl shadow of three-ness — the totally
symmetric state of three doublets. A ternary object wearing a binary-looking dimension.

**What is absent is the tell.** The sign rep of S₃ never appears in (ℂ²)^⊗3 — there is no
totally antisymmetric spin state of three spin-½'s (ℂ² has no room for three antisymmetric
indices). The ternary antisymmetry is exiled into color: ε^{abc}. This is not analogy — it is
the founding argument of QCD. The Δ⁺⁺ (a quartet member: three parallel spins, manifestly
symmetric) is exactly what forced Greenberg / Han–Nambu to introduce threefold color. **The
quartet is historically the object that revealed three.** The framework's own §2.15 (three
Borromean strands carrying color-3) and the ε^{abc} color singlet are the same structure.

## The ironic inversion of this session's impasse

The 2T fragmentation banked in G-2a-S3 — **4 → 2 + 2** on losing the C₄ — is now legible: it is
the ternary Sym³ collapsing back into binary doublets when the four-fold axis is removed. The
octahedral C₄ is what holds the symmetric cube together as one irreducible object; drop to
tetrahedral and it decays into the doubling-algebra reading (two 2's).

So the impasse restates cleanly:

- the quartet is **born of three** (Sym³, S₃-symmetric on three strands),
- but was required to be **held together spatially by a four** (the C₄ → 2O route of §2.85),
- and the Borromean object — the archetypal ternary structure (μ̄₁₂₃ ≠ 0, all pairwise linkings
  zero) — supplies exactly the **ternary** rotations (the C₃'s survive in A₄ = the Borromean
  rotational symmetry, V4.50) while structurally **denying the four-fold** (V4.50/CKS: the
  eccentricity Borromean-ness needs breaks the C₄).

Three-ness gives threes and twos; the §2.85 derivation demanded a literal four. The mismatch
that produced the impasse may therefore be a mismatch of **derivation**, not of **object**.

## The candidate escape (R3) — derive the quartet from EXCHANGE, not spatial symmetry

If the quartet's origin is ternary — statistics on three strands — then its natural derivation
is the quark-model route, which needs **no octahedral symmetry at all**:

1. Color is the totally antisymmetric singlet ε^{abc} on the three strands (canon: §2.15).
2. If the three strands exchange with an overall **fermionic** Finkelstein–Rubinstein phase (the
   soliton is a fermion), the total wavefunction is antisymmetric under strand exchange.
3. Antisymmetric total × antisymmetric color ⇒ the **spin⊗spatial sector is totally symmetric**
   under S₃ ⇒ the spin sector is forced onto the **triv_{S₃} = quartet** branch of Schur–Weyl
   (for the spatially-symmetric ground state).

No C₄, no 2O, no octahedral premise. Only S₃ **exchange** on the three strands + the color sign.
The octahedral requirement — the thing V4.50 and G-2a-S3 closed against in both readings — would
then have been an artifact of the **wrong derivation** of a right object. **This is potentially a
dissolution of the impasse, not a workaround.**

## Honest caveats (what must be earned before promotion past R3)

1. **The nucleon is spin-½, not the quartet.** The same Schur–Weyl decomposition gives the
   *mixed-symmetry* 2's, which are the nucleon (spin-½) branch. The physical baryon octet is
   spin-½; the decuplet (Δ) is spin-3/2. So "the quartet is the symmetric branch" is correct but
   the μ_n derivation is about the **neutron (spin-½)**, whose moment involves the mixed-symmetry
   sector and the SU(6) spin-flavor wavefunction. **How the ledger's μ_n derivation actually
   consumes the factor-of-4 must be checked against §2.85 before claiming the impasse dissolves.**
   It is possible the §2.85 "factor-of-4" is not the Δ quartet at all but a different 4 (e.g. the
   color/flavor 4 of Assignment I vs II) — this must be disambiguated first. **This is the single
   biggest open check.**
2. **Existence ≠ magnitude (M.CW).** Deriving quartet existence from exchange statistics is a
   structural/topological result. Converting it into the neutron magnetic-moment **number** is
   dynamical — the M.CW ceiling applies, and the exchange route does not by itself cross that
   bridge.
3. **Which 4 is §2.85's 4?** Prerequisite to everything: confirm whether the ledger's
   factor-of-4 is (a) the Sym³ spin quartet (this note's reading), (b) the σ₄ half-spinor of
   Spin(6)=ℂ⊗𝕆 (the Assignment I/II object), or (c) the same 4 seen two ways. The Schur–Weyl
   reading is compelling for a spin-3/2 quartet but the ledger's "4" may be the color-4, in which
   case this note re-frames the wrong object. **Prior Address / disambiguation required before
   any compute.**
4. **Suggestive lineage, not proof.** The QCD-history parallel (Δ⁺⁺ → color) is genuine and
   strengthens the reading, but it is motivation, not derivation.
