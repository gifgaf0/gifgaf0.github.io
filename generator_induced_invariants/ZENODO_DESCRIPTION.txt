A public-key construction that builds its public object by applying a fixed family of linear maps to random seeds—to compress the key, to inherit a symmetry, or to support a reduction—produces an object that is the image of a single fixed design matrix. Every invariant of that matrix, its rank first of all, is then imposed on the public object whatever the seeds. When the rank ρ of the design matrix is below the ambient dimension, the public key is distinguishable from uniform by exact linear algebra, and recovering the noise reduces to an LWE instance of dimension at most ρ. The argument takes one line—the rank of a product is at most the rank of either factor—yet the weakness is easy to miss: a rank check on a realized instance of dimension n can certify only ρ ≥ n, so a check run below the ceiling returns a clean result on a broken scheme. We make this concrete with a reproducible case study, a withdrawn sedenion Module-LWE proposal whose public matrix has rank at most 76 at every module rank and whose own structural check, run at module rank 4, could not see the ceiling. We package the lesson as an exact and cheap diagnostic—compute the rank of the design matrix over the working field, then confirm it on an instance larger than the ceiling against a uniform control—with a dependency-free implementation that reproduces the case study, and we place the mechanism among published breaks in which structure introduced for a benefit became the attack surface. The diagnostic is necessary, not sufficient, and costs less than the lattice estimate it should precede.
The case study is the author's own withdrawn proposal; its full break is in the author's negative-result note, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23270248.
Accompanying file: design_matrix_rank.py, the diagnostic the note describes (Python 3.8 or later, no dependencies; SHA-256 bb22d680f7b82e3d69e722923654a87ea4c558cbe9f932ea4c1a0b9a0f401b94). Running python3 design_matrix_rank.py --self-test reproduces the case study's rank table and its rank-76 ceiling.
