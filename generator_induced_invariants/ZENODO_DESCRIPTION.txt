A public-key construction that builds its public object by applying a fixed family of linear maps to random seeds—to compress the key, to inherit a symmetry, or to support a reduction—produces an object that is the image of a single fixed design matrix. The row space of that matrix, and with it its rank, is then imposed on the public object whatever the seeds. When the rank ρ of the design matrix is below the ambient dimension, every public key is rank-deficient: exact linear algebra distinguishes it from the uniform matrix a security reduction assumes, and recovering the noise reduces to an LWE instance of dimension at most ρ. The argument takes one line—the rank of a product is at most the rank of either factor—yet the weakness is easy to miss: a rank check on a realized instance of dimension n can certify only ρ ≥ n, so a check run below the ceiling returns a clean result on a broken scheme. We make this concrete with a reproducible case study, a withdrawn sedenion Module-LWE proposal whose public matrix has rank at most 76 at every module rank and whose own structural check, run at module rank 4, could not see the ceiling. We package the lesson as an exact and cheap diagnostic—count, then compute the rank of the design matrix over the working field at the deployed size, and confirm it on an instance larger than the ceiling—with a dependency-free implementation that reproduces the case study and passes two structured, sound controls, and we place the mechanism among published breaks in which structure introduced for a benefit became the attack surface. Passing the diagnostic is necessary for any security argument that assumes a uniform, full-rank matrix, but not sufficient, and it costs less than the lattice estimate it should precede.
The case study is the author's own withdrawn proposal; its full break is in the author's negative-result note, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23270248.
Accompanying file: design_matrix_rank.py, the diagnostic the note describes (Python 3.8 or later, no dependencies; SHA-256 b6a368c9b9f1e269b9dca93bc0d5f8c81ab5f5797687352fe3c4154e147f9818). Running python3 design_matrix_rank.py --self-test reproduces the case study's rank table and its rank-76 ceiling, re-derives the other facts the note computes, and checks the tool's verdicts on the case study, on two structured controls that pass, and on designs built to fail.
