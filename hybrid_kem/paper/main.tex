\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We give machine-checked computational proofs, in CryptoVerif, for
HYBRID-AKE, a SIGMA-style handshake whose session key comes from an
ephemeral hybrid KEM (X-Wing, or ML-KEM-1024 with X25519 under a
transcript-hashing combiner) and whose authentication comes from composite
ML-DSA/EdDSA signatures. We prove injective agreement in both directions,
real-or-random secrecy of every fresh session key with forward secrecy,
key-compromise impersonation resistance and key reveal, and cross-role key
uniqueness, using only CryptoVerif's post-quantum-sound library. Separately,
we prove that each hybrid primitive meets the assumption the protocol proof
makes on it if either of its components does.

The paper is organised around what this process caught. Mechanization
corrected two of the four results of the pen-and-paper sketch that preceded
it: the composite signature also needs a collision-resistant pre-hash, and
X-Wing relying on X25519 also needs ciphertext second-preimage resistance of
ML-KEM, as the X-Wing paper's classical theorem assumes; the ML-KEM-1024
combiner avoids that assumption because it hashes the ML-KEM ciphertext.
Sixteen mutation tests show which design elements and model guards each proof
depends on. An independent review of our first write-up found claims the
models did not support: a tighter bound than proved, non-standard assumptions
not flagged as such, and a weaker adversary than described.
Comparing the models with the implementation found that our X-Wing raised an
error on low-order X25519 points, where the specification's algorithm returns
a shared secret. The same divergence had been reported for another library;
we fixed ours and characterise the affected inputs completely. All models,
proof scripts and tests are public.
\end{abstract}
