\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We give machine-checked computational proofs, in CryptoVerif, for
HYBRID-AKE, a SIGMA-like handshake whose session key comes from an
ephemeral hybrid KEM (X-Wing, or ML-KEM-1024 with X25519 under a
transcript-hashing combiner) and whose authentication comes from composite
ML-DSA/EdDSA signatures over the transcript. We prove injective agreement in
both directions, real-or-random secrecy of every fresh session key with
forward secrecy, key-compromise impersonation resistance and key reveal, and
cross-role key uniqueness, using only CryptoVerif's post-quantum-sound
library. Separately, we prove that each hybrid primitive meets the
assumption the protocol proof makes on it if either of its components does.

The paper is organised around what this process caught. The pen-and-paper
sketch that preceded the mechanization had dropped two conditions that its
primary sources state: the composite signature also needs a
collision-resistant pre-hash, and X-Wing relying on X25519 also needs
ciphertext second-preimage resistance of ML-KEM. Its authentication bound
also lacked a factor for the number of parties. The ML-KEM-1024 combiner
needs no security assumption on ML-KEM in that direction, because it hashes
the ML-KEM ciphertext. Twenty-two mutation tests, one for each freshness
guard among them, show what each proof depends on. A review of our first
write-up, by a separate instance of the AI assistant used throughout this
work, found claims the models did not support: a tighter bound than proved,
non-standard assumptions not flagged as such, and restrictions on the
adversary disclosed in the notes but not in the result statements. It also noted that our
X-Wing implementation raised an error on low-order X25519 points, where the
specification's algorithm returns a shared secret, a divergence reported
earlier for another library. We fixed it and give a self-contained proof of
which inputs are affected. All models, proof scripts and tests are public.
\end{abstract}
