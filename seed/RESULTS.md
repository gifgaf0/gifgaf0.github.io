# Seed experiments: results

*October 19, 2026. Nine experiments testing SEED.md, run against the preregistration in `PLAN.md` (committed at `0dcd3fe`, before any of them was run). Headline numbers were computed twice, by two agents with separate code, and the second did not read the first one's code until its own numbers were in. The exceptions, computed once, are of two kinds: E8's Q = 3–7 knot minima (relaxed by one code; the cross-check re-evaluated those fields with its own code) and results added during reconciliation (E1's 3,402-run speed test, time-reversal demonstration and revised DSV quanta count; E4's δ-branch enumeration and generic real-coupling variant; a few smaller diagnostics). Disagreements were settled in the open: each experiment folder has its `REPORT.md`, `crosscheck/CROSSCHECK.md` and `RECONCILE.md`.*

## The short answer

No set of experiments can prove a theory of everything, and these don't. What they do show:

1. **The seed's §3 mathematics is right.** Every computable claim in §3 holds in exact arithmetic (E0, 110 checks; the one floating-point step fixes the sign of a Gauss sum, and the other sign would only swap b₇ and b̄₇). One closing sentence gets a wording refinement, not a correction. Two §2 arguments need a premise added: odd Hopf charge for knot fermions, and H⁵(G₂; ℝ) = 0 for the G₂ case.
2. **The three starter models, and §7.5's gravitating skyrmion, behave as published.** The tools reproduce the literature for φ⁴ kinks, Skyrme baryons, Faddeev–Hopf knots and gravitating skyrmions, usually to better than 1% (table below). One published point could not be reproduced: DSV's example pulse-train collision is chaotic and stays OPEN.
3. **Most of the seed's specific claims hold.**
   - *Claims that could have failed and did not:* the lowest SU(3) fold states are the octet and the decuplet; a G₂ fold is always a boson; pair production from colliding packets has a ragged boundary, not a threshold; production can come arbitrarily close to the 2M floor (1.0012 × 2M shown), though only with data tuned beyond 10⁻⁸; knot energy per unit charge falls, with E/Q^{3/4} ≈ 1.2 for Q = 1–7; the Q = 7 knot relaxes to a trefoil (from a trefoil start); a self-gravitating fold exists; the proton is larger than its Compton wavelength.
   - *Checks that hold by construction or by conservation laws,* so they show consistency rather than test nature: one cone; the equivalence principle; energy bends the cone (it is GR; "stress-energy" is the better word); the 2M floor and pairing; exact-SU(3) degeneracy; integer charges and spin ½ for odd N_c, given the imported Wess–Zumino rule.
4. **Several need amending.**
   - "Within roughly 30%" misses g_A by 52%.
   - In 1+1-D φ⁴, packets fold by amplitude, not by energy, and a single tall packet folds on its own.
   - Pulse trains make pairs in a velocity band, not "most easily at low speed".
   - The optimality of the 3/4 power is Lin & Yang's theorem; a fit over Q ≤ 7 is consistent with it but cannot test it.
   - The electron misses the classical-fold size by at least 3.5 × 10⁶; the seed's "at least 10⁵" is true but conservative.
   - Unbroken, the order-21 group allows at most one common mass for three generations in a triplet (no hierarchy); none of its residual-symmetry patterns fits the measured mixing (best lepton χ² 2459, best CKM χ² 1.08 × 10⁵); F₂₁ textures fit only with vacuum alignments put in by hand, and then predict nothing with complex couplings and at most a weak δ_CP constraint (at least 16–22 branches per class) with real couplings.
5. **The places a theory of everything has to reach are far away.** Four of them now have numbers; one is settled by a theorem.
   - **Size.** An electron-like fold misses the size condition by at least 3.5 × 10⁶ (LEP's bound, which is model dependent). It would need Skyrme e ≥ 1.65 × 10⁴ (1.5–2.05 × 10⁴ across radius definitions), far past the point where a fold is a classical shape at all (E7).
   - **Pairs.** QED pair production is a perturbative power law: Breit–Wheeler peaks at 170 mb and scales as α², with no exponential. An illustrative fold-like suppression e^{−F/g²}, with F = 3.84 taken from Demidov & Levkov's (1+1)-D model, would be 10⁻²²⁹ for g² = α, or 10⁻¹⁸ for g² = 4πα. So easy pair production is not evidence for a strongly coupled fold sector. If electrons were folds, the size argument would still require strong coupling, and that sector would then have to reproduce perturbative QED at low energy, which is untested (E7).
   - **The cone.** Weinberg–Witten is a theorem, read here via Porrati, not a measurement: in a Poincaré-invariant theory with a Lorentz-covariant conserved T^μν, no massless |h| = 2 particle exists. Commitment 1 assumes such a tensor for the fold field, so the graviton cannot be a composite of it. Commitment 5's metric must be fundamental, as SEED §5 already takes it, or emergent only through an evasion that strains commitment 1. A neutral composite photon is allowed (E7).
   - **The vacuum.** Its tension is (2.24 meV)⁴, from 10⁴² (physical f_π) to 10¹²³ (unreduced Planck mass) times below natural scales; the fold's own fitted f gives 6.9 × 10⁴¹, the electroweak scale 1.5 × 10⁵⁶ (E7).
   - **Gravity on one fold.** It changes the metric at the core by about 3 × 10⁻³⁹ (E6).

**Where that leaves the seed.** It is an arrangement of known physics (topological baryons as in QCD's Skyrme limit, general relativity, Hopf knots, finite flavor groups) in which each piece works as published. The pieces were tested one at a time, not as one theory, and one cross-piece tension was found: at the fold's own fitted f_π, π⁰ → γγ comes out 2.0–2.6 times too large, which is the square of the fold's 30% f_π deficit (38% for the SU(3) refit; E5). The starter models predict what the known models predict, including their known misses: g_A about 50% low, and exotic 10̄ and 27 multiplets with exotic charges that match no established state. The new outputs either do not describe nature (a G₂ field gives only bosons) or rest on vacuum alignments put in by hand (the PSL(2,7) patterns, which give sin²θ₁₂ ≈ 0.32–0.34 against NuFIT's 0.307). The seed has no decisive prediction of its own yet, and no route yet to the electron, the quark and lepton spectrum, or the size of the vacuum energy. Its 60° structure is confirmed as mathematics; placing it in the internal space is commitment 2, which every experiment adopts as an input and none tests.

## Seed statements not tested

- **Commitments 2 and 3** (the geometry is internal; matter is a fold): every experiment adopts them as inputs.
- **§2:** Breit–Wheeler 1934 and the RHIC measurement of 2021 rest on the literature only (see the corrections below). So do the SU(2) route to spin ½ (π₄(SU(2)) = ℤ₂, Finkelstein–Rubinstein) and π₄(SU(3)) = 0; E2 takes the nucleon's J = ½ as an input. That gravitational waves share the cone was not checked; E5 checks only the photon and the pion.
- **§3:** the physics framing ("moved from space to the internal space, the hexagon costs nothing") and the "one reading to try" are not mathematical claims.
- **§4:** monopoles, and the claim that a theory unifying the forces in one simple group always has them. Of the stabilisers §4 names, only the Skyrme term was used; gauge fields and Q-balls were not. Albayrak & Vachaspati's results with a quantum go-between (wide, energetic, slow packets work best; no kinks without it) rest on the literature: E1 tests only the single-field analogue, so what was refuted is PLAN's E1d analogy, not their claim.
- **§5:** that a massless spin-2 field coupled to all energy is GR, and DESI's drift, are literature (both get corrections below).
- **§8:** which folds are which particles; why the vacuum's tension is so small and whether it drifts (only its size was quantified); whether the 60° is chosen or falls out of something deeper.

## What missed, and what changed on the way

The method only works if misses are kept. These are the ones that matter:

1. **E6, my preregistered prediction was wrong.** I predicted the fold's gravitational binding would equal the Newtonian −½∫ρΦ to 1%. It is 1.29 times that, because the fold's internal stresses gravitate too; the full result, −G∫(ρ + p_r) m/r dV, was derived and reproduced in two gauges. It stays a MISS of the prediction, with the physics explained. The seed's "energy bends the cone" is not touched by it: SEED §5 names GR, whose source is the full stress-energy.
2. **E1, the DSV example point.** The first report called (ω, v) = (0.99, 0.43) reproduced from one run. The cross-check could not reproduce it, and the reconciliation showed why: the point is chaotic. A long-lived central oscillation decides whether to release a pair at t ≈ 850, after even the two finest discretisations have separated, and none of 11 neighbouring initial conditions produces. It is now OPEN; a converged producer sits nearby at (0.99, 0.41).
3. **E1, speed and energy.** The first report said slower packets produce more at matched energy. That was an amplitude effect: at equal energy, slow packets are taller. A 3,402-run test at fixed packet shape, with the decision rule fixed beforehand, finds no slow preference (faster packets produce as much or more), so the preregistered "favours low speed" is REFUTED, and "favours wide packets" is AMENDED (it holds only at fixed amplitude). The first report also said 2M was not the onset of production; that holds only for the families scanned, since time-reversed captures approach 2M.
4. **E4, "no class fits and predicts"** held only for complex couplings, a choice the first report did not state. With real couplings three classes survive, with one weak prediction: δ_CP lies on at least 16–22 discrete branches per class, 25 values in all, spread over the whole circle.
5. **E7, two numbers were wrong.** The Demidov–Levkov exponent was misread by eye as 3.93; read from the vector paths of the published figures it is 3.846. A spline artifact inflated a hopfion radius (2.144; the correct value is 2.061). No conclusion changed.
6. **E8, the absolute knot energies** match Sutcliffe at his grid spacing, but two independent codes put the continuum about 2% above his table.
7. **Vocabulary and provenance.** Several reports first labelled seed predictions or internal checks as CALIBRATED; the reconciliations relabelled them. One target ("36.5 F_π/e = 1.2315 × 3π²", PLAN E2) mixed three slightly different literature values; all three are matched. PLAN's label "[read: Schechter–Weigel, Praszalowicz]" on it and on Λ = 50.9 was wrong: neither number appears in those texts (36.5 and 50.9 are ANW's; Schechter–Weigel give 36.44 and 50.90).
8. **Other numbers corrected in reconciliation.** E4's best PSL(2,7) full-residual lepton χ², 200 → 162 (a δ-wrapping bug); E1e's DSV quanta count, 4.78 → 5.20 m²/λ (the earlier producer was not converged); E6's light-deflection maximum, 18.83 → 18.86 per unit α, its location moved by 5%; E8's infinite-volume E₁, 1.2243 → 1.2248. No verdict changed.

## Deviations that affected a pass/fail call

Each report lists its deviations from PLAN. These are the ones a verdict depended on:

- **E5, g_πNN.** PLAN gave no tolerance. 8.868 against 8.9 passes only under ±0.05, chosen after the run; it fails the magnetic moments' ±0.02 (|Δ| = 0.032).
- **E7, proton M·R/ħc ≈ 2.8.** It holds with the physical 939 MeV (2.83) but not with PLAN's own c₀/e², which uses the classical mass (2.60, outside a ±0.05 set by E7). The row is labelled CONSISTENT, not CALIBRATED.
- **E8, absolute knot energies.** PLAN fixed the table and the 2% tolerance but not whether to compare on the same grid or in the continuum. All seven pass on the same grid (+1.25% to +1.80%). Against the same-box continuum, 4 of 7 miss 2% (Q = 1, 4, 5 and 7), including the required Q = 1 and 4.
- **E8, the finest stages.** The Δx = 0.1 stages for Q ≥ 3 were stopped on the compute budget, short of the 10⁻⁷ convergence criterion; the remaining energy drift is below 10⁻⁶ relative.
- **E4, the δ pull.** The original wrapped δ_CP in a way inconsistent with NuFIT's asymmetric range; the convention was corrected after the fits (no verdict changed). The real-coupling survey that changed the F₂₁ evidence was added after the cross-check.
- **E1, "ragged".** PLAN gave no numerical definition. The operational test (at least 20% of producing lines switching more than once, or at least 3 producing regions of 3 or more points) was fixed after the coarse scan and before the slices were inspected.

## Corrections to SEED.md

The literature check (PLAN §3) found no fabricated or wrong reference, and every volume and page matched. From that check and the experiments, for the next revision:

- **§2, pairs:** twice the fold energy is the exact classical floor, but reaching it takes finely tuned initial data; in the packet families scanned in E1 (1+1-D φ⁴), production starts at 1.86 × 2M (Gaussian packets) or higher (7.8 × 2M for DSV trains).
- **§2, Breit–Wheeler at RHIC:** STAR 2021 is real (6085 exclusive pairs in ultra-peripheral Au+Au), but the photons are quasi-real, whether it counts as Breit–Wheeler is debated, and it was not the first observation of γγ → e⁺e⁻ in heavy-ion collisions.
- **§2, knots:** Hopf folds can be fermions only for odd Hopf charge (Krusch & Speight 2006).
- **§2, G₂:** the bosons-only result also follows from representation theory alone (E3), and the no-WZ argument as written needs H⁵(G₂; ℝ) = 0 as well as π₄ = π₅ = 0.
- **§3, last paragraph (a wording refinement; E0 finds every clause true):** "they stack as G₂ ⊃ SU(3)⋊ℤ₂ ⊃ PGL(2,7) ⊃ PSL(2,7) ⊃ F₂₁, but the split into two Fano families survives only up to PSL(2,7); the G₂ turns that complete p6m swap the families." The seed's flipped copy of PSL(2,7) is exactly LNR's published basis, so "Klein's own copy" is a labelling convention.
- **§4, packets (1+1-D φ⁴, E1):** a packet folds when its amplitude is large enough (A ≳ 0.4), not when its energy is; a single tall packet folds on its own. DSV's trains make pairs in a velocity band that moves to lower speed as the pulses weaken, not "most easily at low speed".
- **§4, Albayrak & Vachaspati:** the quantum-mediation paper is 2024 (preprint 2023). The axes and the "gaps even at high energy" wording given for the 2026 paper could not be confirmed; its abstract says production is fractured, "with holes and isolated regions".
- **§4, the 3/4 power:** its optimality is Lin & Yang (2004); Sutcliffe 2007 is the numerical agreement, and a fit over Q ≤ 7 is consistent with 3/4 but cannot test it.
- **§5:** Einstein's equations from a massless spin-2 field are Weinberg 1965 (Phys. Rev. 138, B988) and Deser 1970; Weinberg 1964 proves universal coupling. A suggested wording: "stress-energy bends the cone" (no change of physics; E6). For DESI, a DES-Dovekie recalibration lowers the 4.2σ supernova figure to about 3.2σ.
- **§6B:** "within roughly 30%" → "about 30–35% for the magnetic moments, g_πNN and f_π, within 25% for the isoscalar radii and better for ratios, about 50% for g_A; the proton and neutron charge radii need a pion mass" (E5). Add that the same rotor next predicts exotic multiplets, 10̄ (J = ½) and 27 (J = 3/2), about 600–770 MeV above the octet and carrying exotic charges (Θ⁺, Ξ⁻⁻, Q = ±2), which match no established state (E2, E5).
- **§7.3:** the order-21 group restricts couplings, but unbroken it allows at most one common mass for three generations in a triplet, none of its residual-symmetry patterns fits the measured mixing, and textures that fit need vacuum alignments put in by hand.
- **§8:** the electron misses by at least 3.5 × 10⁶ (LEP, a model-dependent bound), not 10⁵. The QCD-baryon analogy does not reach: the baryon has M R/ħc ≈ 2.6–4, the electron needs ≤ 2.85 × 10⁻⁷. Easy electron-pair production reflects weak, perturbative QED, not a strongly coupled fold sector, so the size and coupling items do not point the same way; if electrons are folds, the strongly coupled sector must still give perturbative QED at low energy. Weinberg–Witten excludes a composite graviton under commitment 1, but not a neutral composite photon.
- **§9:** Albayrak & Vachaspati, Phys. Rev. D 109, 036001 is 2024; DESI DR2 II is Phys. Rev. D 112, 083515 (2025); Vakulenko & Kapitanskii's original is Dokl. Akad. Nauk SSSR 246, 840. Add Lin & Yang (2004), Krusch & Speight (2006), Weinberg (1965) and Schael et al. (2013, the LEP bound).

## Where to push next

Ranked by how much each would tell:

1. **The electron.** No classical fold of the kinds tested can make it (E7: a theorem for one-scale soliton families, checked for the Skyrme model with and without a pion mass and for the hopfion; the other stabilisers of §4, gauge fields and Q-balls, were not tested). The question is whether any quantum, strongly coupled fold sector yields a light, pointlike fermion that couples perturbatively with α = 1/137 at low energy, or whether fermions must enter as fundamental fields with folds as composites. This is the deciding problem.
2. **A testable output of the seed's PSL(2,7).** Three ℤ₂-partial residual-symmetry patterns of PSL(2,7) fit current neutrino data (E4, in an analysis added beyond the plan): 3 of 12 classes pass, at p = 0.34, 0.28 and 0.022. Each has 2 parameters for 4 observables. They give s₁₂² ≈ 0.32–0.34 against NuFIT 6.0's 0.307, and a precise s₁₂² measurement is the direct test. Their δ_CP values (253°, 284°, 235°) are outputs of fits that include NuFIT's δ, and lie outside its 1σ range; DUNE and Hyper-Kamiokande would add to the test. Two of the three are the trimaximal patterns TM1 and TM2, which PSL(2,7)'s S₄ and A₄ subgroups already give; only the ℤ₇ class needs PSL(2,7) itself. All three need a dynamical reason for a vacuum alignment that is now put in by hand, and a reason for folds to carry a family index. F₂₁ alone cannot do this: it has no ℤ₂.
3. **Quantise the knots.** Finkelstein–Rubinstein quantisation of the Hopf folds (fermions only at odd Q) and their excitation spectrum; and, with a second code and more than one start per charge, a search for the global minima at Q = 3–7.
4. **The G₂ fold's global minimum.** Only local stability of the embedded fold was shown (E3); a full 3D minimisation in G₂ would settle it.
5. **Pair production beyond one dimension.** E1's ragged boundary is in 1+1 dimensions. Albayrak & Vachaspati (2026) already report fractured vortex production in two dimensions with a quantum mediator; what remains open is single-field production, without a mediator, in two and three dimensions (vortices and skyrmions from colliding packets).
6. **The vacuum energy.** No experiment in this series can touch it; it needs a mechanism, not a simulation.

## How this was done

- **Preregistration.** `PLAN.md` fixed the experiments, targets and pass rules before any computation (commit `0dcd3fe`). Deviations are listed in each report; those that decided a verdict are listed above.
- **Literature.** Publisher sites, arXiv.org and INSPIRE were blocked by the sandbox's proxy. Full texts came from the public arXiv mirror on Google Cloud Storage and numbers from the PDG 2026 database; targets marked [summary] came from search extracts. Several primary sources were never read: Adkins, Nappi & Witten 1983 (its numbers came via Acus et al., Schechter–Weigel and Rakhimov), Zajac 2009 and Bizon–Chmaj 1992 (so E6's α_crit target is [summary]), Weinberg–Witten 1980 (Porrati's re-derivation was used), the ATLAS of Finite Groups and Klein 1878. E2 found PLAN's [read] label on the 36.5 and 50.9 targets overstated (item 7 above). `prior_address/` holds the raw literature check.
- **Two computations each.** A run agent implemented each experiment; an independent agent recomputed the headline numbers with different methods (different solvers, discretisations, representations, exact versus floating arithmetic) before reading the original code; a third reconciled any disagreement. All three outputs are kept. Computed once, and so not independently checked:
  - E8's Q = 3–7 knot minima, the trefoil among them. One code relaxed them; the cross-check re-evaluated those fields (energies, charges, types) with its own code but relaxed only Q = 1 and 2 itself.
  - Results added during reconciliation: E1's 3,402-run speed test (which carries the REFUTED verdict), its time-reversal demonstration (which shows that production can approach 2M arbitrarily closely) and its revised DSV quanta count (5.20 m²/λ); E4's generic real-coupling variant and the δ-branch enumeration of the three real-coupling survivors (the survivors themselves were found by the cross-check and confirmed in reconciliation); and smaller diagnostics (E3's level assignment at R = 40, E6's zero-crossing study, E8's coarse-grid linking numbers and test relaxations).
- **Interruptions.** Two container restarts and three account usage-limit stops interrupted the run. Work resumed from checkpoints on disk. The experiment files record resumed work where it happened: the E2, E4, E5 and E8 runs (container restarts), E1's run (third attempt), the E1 and E8 reconciliations, the earlier cross-check attempts of E1, E2, E4 and E6, and E3's resumed pipeline log (in its `RECONCILE.md`). The usage-limit stops are recorded only here.
- **Compute.** Four shared cores. E8's knot relaxations took about 5 hours of wall time over all grids, about 2.6 of them at Sutcliffe's 151³ grid (one of those stages on two threads), plus 40 minutes for the axisymmetric code. E1's scans took about 105 minutes, plus 85 minutes of reconciliation, on two worker processes. Every other pipeline reruns in under 40 minutes on one core; E4's texture fits, about 27 minutes, are the longest part.
