## Method
**Arithmetic.** No decision is made in floating point.
- **The field Q(zeta), zeta = e^{2 pi i/7}** (`exact_fields.Z7`).
  - Elements are stored as (c0..c5)/d with integer entries, in the basis 1, zeta, ..., zeta^5, and reduced with zeta^6 = -(1+...+zeta^5). This is the same as working modulo x^6+...+1.
  - Complex conjugation is zeta -> zeta^-1.
  - i/sqrt7 is written exactly through the Gauss sum G = (z+z^2+z^4) - (z^3+z^5+z^6). The code checks G^2 = -7 exactly. One float comparison fixes the sign, G = +i sqrt7. Then i/sqrt7 = G/7 and b7 = z+z^2+z^4 = (-1+G)/2.
- **Matrices and groups.**
  - Products, determinants, unitarity (M M^dagger = 1) and traces are exact.
  - Groups come from breadth-first closure with exact hash keys.
  - The Cayley table is built exactly by composing generator permutations along words.
  - Conjugacy classes, orders, normal closures, normalisers and the whole subgroup lattice come from that table.
- **A5** is built over Q(sqrt5) from two rotations of the icosahedron (0, ±1, ±phi): the 3-fold rotation about (1,1,1) and the 2-fold rotation about the edge midpoint (1, phi^2, phi).
- **Lattices, Weyl and affine Weyl groups** use Fractions. **Fano planes, triangulations and the projective line P^1(F7)** use integers.

**Conventions** (as in hexagon_check.py):
- Torus: theta -> diag(exp 2 pi i theta) on theta1+theta2+theta3 = 0. Its kernel is Lambda = Z^3 ∩ plane, with basis b1 = (1,-1,0), b2 = (0,1,-1).
- Seven points: j p with p = (1,2,-3)/7, which corresponds to D^j.
- 120 deg turn: theta -> roll(theta), which is C^-1 D C = D^4.
- Triangles of Lambda + Zp, with a = p and c = p + roll(p): "up" = {x, x+a, x+c} and "down" = {x+a, x+c, x+a+c}.
- Angles are measured from u = (1,-1,0) toward w = (1,1,-2) and decided exactly via cos = (gu.u)/2 and 2 sqrt3 sin = gu.w.
- G2 roots: long e_i - e_j, short e_i - (1,1,1)/3.
- Affine reflections are s_{alpha,k}(x) = x - ((alpha,x) - k) alpha^vee. They are generated by s1 (alpha1 short), s2 (alpha2 long) and s0 = s_{theta,1}, with theta = 3 alpha1 + 2 alpha2 = (0,1,-1).

## Findings
1. **Every §3 computation reproduces exactly.** The float script's rounding-based closure and its random-vector quartic test hide no error. The exact orders are 21, 168 and 168, and the quartics are invariant under every element as polynomial identities.
2. **Characters.**
   - PSL(2,7): chi3 = (3,-1,0,1,b7,b7bar) on classes of size 1, 21, 56, 42, 24, 24.
   - F21: chi3 = (3,0,0,b7,b7bar).
   - In both, chi(D) = b7 = (-1+i sqrt7)/2, ⟨chi,chibar⟩ = 0 and nu = 0, so both triplets are complex.
   - A5: both triplets take the values (3,-1,0,(1±sqrt5)/2) and have nu = +1. The remaining irrep degrees are forced to be 4 and 5, so A5 has exactly two triplets and both are real.
   - PSL(2,7) has 6 classes and is perfect. Given 1, 3 and 3bar, the remaining degrees are forced to be (6,7,8), so 3 and 3bar are its only triplets.
3. **The flipped copy is LNR's published basis.**
   - LNR's A = Rf and AB = D, and LNR's <A,B> is the same set of 168 matrices.
   - LNR's "Klein quartic" is the mirror form.
   - The order-7 diagonal symmetries of x^3y+y^3z+z^3x are exactly the powers of Dk; those of xy^3+yz^3+zx^3 are exactly the powers of D.
   - So "Klein's own copy" is a coordinate-labelling convention.
4. **The PSL(2,7) subgroup lattice has 179 subgroups in 15 classes.** The list is complete: it is closed under adding any element, and every subgroup is reached from {1} that way. The maximal subgroups match ATLAS, and F21 is one of the 8 of order 21.
5. **G2's affine Weyl group is p6m.**
   - It is Lambda ⋊ D6, and p6m is the only wallpaper group with point group of order 12.
   - Its Wyckoff sites are 1a (6mm), 2b (3m) and 3c (2mm).
   - SU(3)'s affine Weyl group is p3m1.
   - The seven points reduce p6m to p6 and p3m1 to p3. No point j ≠ 0 lies on a mirror.
6. **New: the 7-vertex torus is unique and chiral.**
   - An exhaustive search over all 7-vertex surfaces with chi = 0 finds 120 labelled copies in one orbit and no Klein bottle.
   - Aut = AGL(1,7) of order 42, and every automorphism preserves orientation. That is why no mirror keeps the seven points.
   - Each SU(3) mirror carries <D>'s points to those of diag(z,z^4,z^2).
   - The search does not cover 7-vertex spheres and projective planes. Both exist; one example of each is built and checked.
7. **New: the construction is essentially unique.** Only 12 of the 48 order-7 torus points give a hexagonal finer lattice. They form two subgroups, which the SU(3) mirrors swap.
8. **New: the groups stack into a chain.**
   - The PSL(2,7) in U(3) containing <D,C> is unique. The argument now uses only computed facts:
     - the order-21 subgroups form one class, and N(F21) = F21;
     - |Aut F21| = 42, and every automorphism is induced by N_PGL(2,7)(F21), which has order 42 and trivial centraliser;
     - 3 and 3bar are the only triplets;
     - F21's triplet is irreducible, and chi(D) = b7 ≠ b7bar.

     So any such copy agrees with the flipped copy on F21, has the same character, and by Schur's lemma is the same group.
   - The flipped copy is closed under complex conjugation kappa.
   - kappa is an octonion automorphism with e -> -e. It acts as the 180 deg G2 turn (x->6x), which swaps the families.
   - <flipped PSL(2,7), kappa> has order 336 and is explicitly isomorphic to PGL(2,F7) acting on P^1(F7) = F7 ∪ {∞}.
     - Under this isomorphism the seven torus labels sit in F7, and D -> x+1, C -> 2x, Rf -> -1/x, kappa -> -x.
     - kappa, kappa C and kappa C^-1 are the 180, 300 and 60 deg G2 turns, so all the point-keeping G2 turns lie in this PGL(2,7) ⊂ G2.
   - The two Fano families are exactly the collineation groups obtained from PSL(2,7) acting on its two S4 classes (points and lines). kappa swaps them.
   - On the labels, <D,C,kappa> gives all of AGL(1,7) = Aut(triangulation).
9. **Extra: G2 ⊃ SU(3).**
   - Der(O) = g2: dim 14, compact, rank <= 2.
   - The stabiliser of e is su(3) (dim 8), spanned by su(3) acting on C^3.
   - kappa normalises the torus and acts as -1, so W(G2) = W(SU(3)) × {±1}. This confirms hexagon_check.py's model of W(G2).
